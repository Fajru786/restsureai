Understanding Sleep Disorders

Learn what sleep is, why sleep disorders matter, their causes, symptoms, and how RestSure AI helps in early detection and awareness.

What Is Sleep?

Sleep is a natural and essential biological process during which the brain and body remain active while restoring energy, supporting learning and memory, and maintaining overall health. During sleep, the brain cycles through different stages of non-rapid eye movement (non-REM) and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep.

Both the duration and quality of sleep are important. Interrupted or poor-quality sleep can affect physical health, emotional well-being, concentration, and daily functioning.

Why Sleep Disorders Matter

Sleep disorders are conditions that disturb normal sleep patterns, affecting how well, how long, and when you sleep. Many people live with undiagnosed sleep problems that negatively impact mood, productivity, immunity, and long-term health.

Common Types of Sleep Disorders

Insomnia

Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking up too early.

  • Takes more than 30 minutes to fall asleep
  • Frequent night awakenings
  • Feeling tired after waking
Sleep Apnea

A breathing-related disorder where airflow stops repeatedly during sleep.

  • Loud snoring
  • Breathing pauses during sleep
  • Daytime sleepiness
Restless Leg Syndrome

A neurological condition causing an uncontrollable urge to move your legs.

  • Creeping, crawling, or tingling sensations
  • Symptoms worsen in the evening or at night
  • Relief temporarily achieved by moving or stretching
Narcolepsy

A chronic disorder that affects the brain's ability to control sleep-wake cycles.

  • Overwhelming daytime drowsiness
  • Sudden, uncontrollable sleep attacks
  • Sleep paralysis or sudden loss of muscle control

What Causes Sleep Disorders?

Medical conditions such as heart, lung, or nerve disorders

Mental health issues like stress, anxiety, and depression

Caffeine, alcohol, or medication use

Irregular schedules and aging

Common Symptoms

  • Frequent difficulty falling or staying asleep
  • Daytime fatigue or excessive sleepiness
  • Snoring, gasping, or choking during sleep
  • Tingling or urge to move legs at night
  • Temporary inability to move when waking up

How Are Sleep Disorders Diagnosed?

Diagnosis often involves reviewing medical history, sleep habits, and physical examination. In some cases, a sleep study (polysomnogram) is conducted to monitor brain activity, breathing patterns, heart rate, and muscle movements during sleep.

Treatment & Management

Healthy sleep habits and lifestyle changes

Therapy and relaxation techniques

Medical devices or short-term medication
